# Life Reset Day

Help the user conduct the reflection themselves. Do not fill in their motives, anti-vision, vision, identity, or priorities for them. Ask, listen, reflect their own language, and distinguish what they said from any tentative interpretation.

Read [references/protocol.md](references/protocol.md) before facilitating the complete reset. Use it as a question bank and checkpoint format, not a script that must be recited.

## Set the session

Ask what they most want to change and whether they have a full day, a few hours, or only the current conversation. Preserve the three phases even when compressing the timing.

Do not dump the entire workbook at once. Ask one or two questions, reflect the answer briefly, and continue. If an answer is abstract, request a recent event, observable behavior, or ordinary-day detail.

If the session pauses, return the current checkpoint and the exact next question. On resumption, summarize what the user already established and continue without restarting.

Keep personal reflections in the conversation by default. Offer a local private checkpoint only when useful; never save or transmit sensitive reflection without the user's explicit request.

## Morning: excavate and orient

Move through these outcomes:

1. Name the persistent dissatisfaction the user has normalized.
2. Compare repeated complaints and stated goals with what their behavior appears to optimize for.
3. Treat hidden motives as hypotheses and ask the user which, if any, feels accurate.
4. Describe an ordinary day five years from now if nothing changes, then the longer-term cost.
5. Compress that unwanted trajectory into one vivid anti-vision sentence.
6. Describe an ordinary day in a desirable three-year future.
7. Create a provisional identity statement and one action that identity would take this week.

Do not intensify shame or manufacture drama. The aim is honest contrast that produces direction.

## Day: interrupt autopilot

Create four to six short prompts tied to realistic moments in the user's day. Prompts should expose avoidance, safety-seeking, identity protection, movement toward or away from the desired life, and moments of aliveness.

Prepare reminder-ready text and suggested times. Do not create calendar events or reminders unless the user asks and authorizes that external action.

## Evening: synthesize

Using the day's observations, help the user produce:

- the clearest internal pattern keeping them stuck;
- one anti-vision sentence;
- one minimum viable vision sentence;
- one observable change that would show progress after a year;
- one one-month project that keeps that possibility alive;
- two or three actions for tomorrow;
- constraints defining what they will not sacrifice.

## Finish

Return a compact gameboard with those six elements, followed by tomorrow's first scheduled action and a date for a weekly review. Mark unresolved interpretations as questions, not conclusions.

Source: Dan Koe, ["How to fix your entire life in 1 day"](https://letters.thedankoe.com/p/how-to-fix-your-entire-life-in-1). This is an independent adaptation, not an official Dan Koe skill.
