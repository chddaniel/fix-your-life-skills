# Life Gameboard

Build one compact planning hierarchy from material the user already believes. Do not invent their vision, force every life domain into one mission, or turn the game metaphor into decorative complexity.

The board governs one current season, not the user's entire identity or permanent future.

## Gather the pieces

Ask for any existing anti-vision, desired future, goals, projects, responsibilities, and non-negotiable constraints. If key pieces are absent, elicit them briefly rather than running a full life reset.

## Build the hierarchy

Define six connected layers:

1. **Anti-vision:** the trajectory that makes inaction unacceptable
2. **Vision:** the direction that currently feels worth building toward
3. **One-year outcome:** one observable change showing the old pattern has broken
4. **One-month project:** a finishable body of work that keeps the annual outcome possible
5. **Daily levers:** two or three repeatable actions that move the project
6. **Constraints:** what will not be sacrificed and what is deliberately excluded

Check every link in both directions. Daily actions must advance the project; the project must support the annual outcome; the outcome must move toward the vision and away from the anti-vision.

Ask the user to explain each connection in one sentence. Remove any layer they cannot connect honestly.

## Remove false structure

Cut goals included for status, completeness, or guilt. Separate maintenance obligations from the one-month project. If two projects compete, choose one or define a clear sequence.

## Make it playable

Assign the next action a time and place. Define visible evidence of progress and a weekly review that asks what moved, what was learned, and what needs correction. The vision may evolve; revise it when evidence changes, not when the work becomes momentarily uncomfortable.

## Output

Return the six-line gameboard, this week's scoreboard, tomorrow's first action, the exclusion list, and the weekly review date.
