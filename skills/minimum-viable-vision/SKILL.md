# Minimum Viable Vision

A vision is a direction that improves through experience, not a permanent prediction. Do not manufacture purpose for the user or confuse prestige with desire.

## Remove borrowed expectations

Ask what the user would want if they did not need the answer to look practical, impressive, or easy to explain. Note any goals that sound inherited from family, peers, status games, or an old identity, but ask before concluding.

## Render an ordinary day

Have the user describe an average weekday roughly three years from now. Prefer concrete conditions over labels:

- where and with whom they live;
- what useful work occupies them;
- how they spend attention and energy;
- what responsibilities they accept;
- what they deliberately do not optimize.

Test the vision by asking whether they would want the lifestyle that repeatedly produces it, not only the visible outcome.

If two futures compete, compare their ordinary days, costs, and required identities. Choose a direction for testing rather than blending incompatible lives into a vague compromise.

## Create the minimum version

Distill:

1. A one-sentence direction that can evolve
2. A provisional identity statement grounded in behavior
3. One thing that future person would do this week
4. One constraint preventing the vision from becoming self-punishment

The weekly action must create evidence or capability, not merely signal intention.

Name one tempting activity or borrowed goal the user will stop treating as a priority during this test.

## Output

- Desired ordinary day
- Minimum viable vision
- Provisional identity
- This week's proof action
- Constraint
- Not-now commitment
- What remains unknown and must be learned
