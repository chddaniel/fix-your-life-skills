# Identity Loop Audit

Treat identity as a working story that influences attention and behavior, not a diagnosis or an essence. Help the user inspect the loop without telling them who they are.

## Map one loop

Ask for a limiting identity statement in the form "I am the kind of person who..." Then trace one recent cycle:

1. The goal or threat active in the situation
2. What the user noticed and ignored
3. The action they took
4. The feedback or relief they received
5. The identity conclusion they drew
6. How they defended or repeated that conclusion

Keep every step tied to the user's example. Mark missing steps as unknown.

Ask for evidence both supporting and contradicting the identity story. If the story changes across settings, identify the conditions rather than treating the label as global.

## Locate its origin and present function

Ask where the story may have come from: repeated experience, family, peers, work, culture, or a past survival strategy. Origin is context, not destiny.

Then ask what consistency the identity protects today. Look for belonging, predictability, status, innocence, or freedom from judgment without presuming any one answer.

## Choose a break point

Do not ask the user to adopt a grand replacement identity they do not believe. Define the smallest credible counterexample they can enact within a week. Phrase the provisional identity as evidence to collect, not positive affirmation.

Example shape: "For the next seven days, I am testing whether I can be someone who ___."

## Output

Provide:

- Old identity story
- Evidence-backed loop
- Evidence against the story
- What the story protects
- Weakest break point
- Seven-day counterexample
- Evidence that would update the story
